About Orbiscribe

Orbiscribe turns any drawing into a chain of spinning circles. Trace a closed shape with your finger or mouse, and Orbiscribe breaks it down with a discrete Fourier transform into circles, each turning at a whole-number speed. Joined tip to tail, they spin together, and the end of the chain redraws your shape.

It's one of the most beautiful ways to see what a Fourier series really does. With only a few circles you get a rough, wobbly outline. As you add more, the tip hugs your line ever more closely, because each extra circle adds a finer level of detail. The largest circles always come first, and a spectrum view shows how much each frequency contributes.

Not in the mood to draw? Try the built-in shapes: a heart, a star, a trefoil, a square or the π symbol. You can change the number of circles and the speed, show or hide the circles and the original drawing, and pause to study a single moment. It's a great classroom demo for Fourier analysis, signal processing or complex numbers, and a mesmerising toy for anyone else.

How to use it

  1. Draw one continuous closed shape on the canvas, or pick a preset shape.
  2. Watch the spinning circles redraw it.
  3. Use the Circles slider to add or remove circles and see the fit change.
  4. Adjust Speed, and toggle the circles or the original line.
  5. Check the Spectrum to see how strong each frequency is.
