Power & voltage

How these relate

dBm = 10·log₁₀(P / 1 mW). 0 dBm is 1 mW, +30 dBm is 1 W, and every +3 dB doubles the power.

Voltages only mean something once you fix an impedance: P = V²rms / Z₀. Into 50 Ω, 0 dBm ≈ 224 mVrms ≈ 107 dBµV.

What these numbers mean

When a load isn't exactly Z₀, some of the wave bounces back. |Γ| is the voltage fraction that reflects. VSWR = (1+|Γ|)/(1−|Γ|) and RL = −20·log₁₀|Γ|.

S11 in dB is just −RL. Mismatch loss is the power you lose to reflection: −10·log₁₀(1−|Γ|²). A VSWR of 2:1 sounds bad but only costs about 0.5 dB.

How an L-match works

Two lossless parts, one in series and one in shunt, are enough to turn any load with R > 0 into a resistive Z₀. On the chart, a series part moves the point along a circle of constant resistance. A shunt part moves it along a circle of constant conductance. The orange path shows the two moves that end at the centre.

Up to four solutions exist. Pick by part availability, by DC path (a shunt L shorts DC to ground), and by whether you need low-pass or high-pass behaviour. Values are ideal and assume lossless parts.

Why the first stage matters most

Friis: F = F₁ + (F₂−1)/G₁ + (F₃−1)/(G₁G₂) + …. Gain early in the chain makes later noise matter less. That is why the LNA goes up front and why loss before it adds straight to the system NF.

Linearity works the other way. Gain before a stage makes that stage's IP3 matter more: 1/IIP3 = 1/IIP3₁ + G₁/IIP3₂ + …. Too much front-end gain buys a low NF at the cost of a poor IIP3. SFDR measures that trade-off.

Noise floor = −174 dBm/Hz + 10·log₁₀(BW) + NF.

Assumptions

Free-space path loss: FSPL = 20·log₁₀(d km) + 20·log₁₀(f MHz) + 32.44. This assumes clear line of sight. Use Extra loss for walls, foliage or rain. Indoors, real loss can be 20–40 dB higher.

Keep at least 60% of the first Fresnel zone free of obstacles. Its radius at mid-path is shown, so you can check whether your mast height is enough.
